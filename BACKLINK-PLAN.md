# Backlink plan for 4kaustraliaiptv.com

Backlinks are links from other websites to yours. They cannot be added from inside the site, so this plan lists what the site now offers to earn them and what to do outside the site. Everything here is white-hat: no bought links, no link farms, no comment spam. Those can get the whole domain penalised.

## 1. Pages built to attract links
| Page | Why people link to it |
|---|---|
| `/tools/iptv-bandwidth-calculator/` | A free, useful tool. Tools earn the most natural links. |
| `/guides/how-much-internet-speed-for-iptv-australia/` | Answers a question people search every day. |
| `/guides/best-dns-for-iptv-australia/` | Practical how-to, easy to cite. |
| `/guides/iptv-epg-guide-explained-australia/` | Troubleshooting guide, often cited on forums. |
| `/best-iptv-australia/`, `/iptv-box-australia/`, `/iptv-player-australia/` | Buyer's guides that comparison and review sites can reference. |

Every one of these has a share row (WhatsApp, Facebook, X, copy link), Open Graph tags and structured data, so shared links look good.

## 2. First 30 days (do these first)
1. **Search Console and Analytics.** Add `4kaustraliaiptv.com` as a Domain property, submit `https://4kaustraliaiptv.com/sitemap.xml`, and paste your GA4 ID into `assets/js/app.js` (`GA_MEASUREMENT_ID`). Without data you cannot tell which links work.
2. **Video.** Record a 3 to 5 minute setup video for each device (Fire TV Stick, Android TV, Samsung, LG). Put the matching guide link in the description. YouTube links are nofollow, but they send real visitors and get the guide seen.
3. **Answer questions honestly.** On Reddit (r/australia tech threads, r/cordcutters), Whirlpool forums, Facebook groups and Quora, answer buffering and setup questions. Link to a guide only when it directly answers the question, and say you run the site. Never post the same link repeatedly.
4. **Google Business Profile** is only for businesses with a real local presence. Skip it unless you have one.

## 3. Outreach (30 to 90 days)
- **Resource pages.** Search Google for: `"NBN speed" guide resources`, `"how much internet speed do I need" streaming`, `cord cutting Australia resources`. If a page lists helpful tools and your calculator is not on it, send a short email offering it.
- **Tech and cord-cutting bloggers.** Offer the calculator or a guide as a useful addition to an existing article. Be specific about what is new.
- **Journalist requests.** Sign up to free expert-request services (for example Qwoted, Featured, Help a B2B Writer) and respond only to questions about streaming, NBN or cord cutting that you can really answer.
- **Local tech communities and newsletters.** Pitch the bandwidth calculator as a free tool.

Keep emails to four or five lines: who you are, what the page is, why it helps their readers, one link. Do not send bulk templates.

## 4. Content that keeps earning links
- Publish one useful guide or tool each month (ideas: NBN speed by suburb type, Wi-Fi vs cable test, how to read your ISP's typical evening speed, a seasonal sports-on-IPTV checklist).
- Update guides when things change and update the date. Fresh, accurate pages get cited more.
- Add original data where you can (for example anonymised results from the calculator).

## 5. Things to avoid
- Buying links, PBNs, or link exchange schemes.
- Comment and forum spam, or the same anchor text everywhere.
- Directories that accept any site for a fee.
- Promising content you cannot license. Sites about unlicensed streaming attract takedowns and bad links; keep the site focused on legal setup help and licensed content.

## 6. How to measure
In Search Console, open **Links** monthly. Track: number of referring domains, which pages get linked, and the anchor text. A realistic target for a new site is a handful of good referring domains in the first three months, then steady growth.
